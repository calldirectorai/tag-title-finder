Title transfer after a private sale

Bought a car from a person, not a dealer? In Maryland the buyer handles the title paperwork. Here is what the MVA asks for, and where to get it done nearby.

Unlike a dealer, a private seller does not file anything for you. The MVA says the buyer is responsible for titling the vehicle. You can do it at an MVA branch, by mail, or at a private tag and title agency, which charges its own service fee on top of the state charges.

What the MVA asks for

  • The original Maryland title, signed over to you, with every required section filled in
  • Any lien listed on the title released, with the lien release
  • A completed Certificate of Title application (form VR-005)
  • Proof of the purchase price, and a bill of sale if it applies to your sale
  • An odometer disclosure, if it applies
  • A Maryland Safety Inspection Certificate (most used vehicles need one)
  • Valid identification
  • Maryland insurance on the vehicle before it is registered
  • Payment for the titling tax and fees

Bill-of-sale rules for some sales have been changing, so check the MVA page or ask the agency whether your sale needs a notarized bill of sale.

Summarized from the Maryland MVA, checked September 2026: Titling a Vehicle Bought from a Private Party · Buying a Vehicle. Requirements change and depend on your situation; the MVA's pages are the final word.

Before you call an agency, ask

  • Do you handle private-sale title transfers?
  • What is your service fee, and what is the total including state charges?
  • Can you title and register the car in the same visit?
  • My car hasn't passed inspection yet. Can you title it now and do temporary tags or plates later?
  • Do I need an appointment, or can I walk in?
